Self-Assessment

The Wheel of Life: How to Find Balance and Set Better Goals

By the New.Vision editorial teamUpdated November 16, 20267 min read

What the Wheel of Life is, where it came from, how to score it honestly, and how to turn your lowest area into a 30-day action plan.

Key takeaways
  • The Wheel of Life is a quick self-assessment of satisfaction across eight life areas.
  • Score honestly and use the extremes — a wheel of all 6s tells you nothing.
  • Don't try to fix everything. Pick the one area that would lift the others.

What is the Wheel of Life?

The Wheel of Life is a simple coaching tool: a circle divided into slices, each representing an area of life. You rate your satisfaction in each from 1 to 10, shade the slices, and instantly see how "round" — or bumpy — your life feels right now. The concept is widely credited to Paul J. Meyer, founder of the Success Motivation Institute, in the 1960s, and it has since become a staple of life coaching.

The eight areas

Versions differ, but most include something like: Career, Money, Health, Relationships, Family & Friends, Personal Growth, Fun & Recreation, and Home & Environment. Some coaches add spirituality or contribution — use the areas that make sense for you.

How to score honestly

  1. Rate satisfaction, not success. A high-earning job you hate might be a 3.
  2. Use your gut. Spend no more than 10 seconds per area.
  3. Avoid the safe middle. If everything is a 5 or 6, push yourself: is it really closer to a 4 or a 7?
  4. Score today, not your best week.

How to read your wheel

Look for three things. The overall size shows general satisfaction. The shape shows balance: a very uneven wheel "bumps" along, often feeling stressful even if some areas are excellent. The gaps show where a small change might have an outsized effect.

Ask yourself: Which area, if it improved by two points, would make other areas better too? Often it's health (more energy for everything) or money (less stress everywhere).

Turn your lowest area into a 30-day plan

  1. Write what a "+2" would look like in that area. Be concrete.
  2. Choose one keystone habit that would move it — something you can do in 20 minutes or less.
  3. Write an if-then plan: "If it's 7am on a weekday, then I'll ___."
  4. Track it daily for 30 days, then re-score your wheel.

Habit research suggests automaticity builds gradually — in one study it took a median of about 66 days for a new daily behaviour to reach its plateau, with large individual differences (Lally et al., 2010). So be patient; 30 days is a strong start, not the finish line.

How often to repeat it

Quarterly is a good rhythm. Save your wheel image each time and you'll have a visual record of how your life is changing.

Frequently asked questions

Who created the Wheel of Life?

It is commonly attributed to Paul J. Meyer of the Success Motivation Institute in the 1960s.

Is a perfect wheel of 10s the goal?

No. Life is seasonal. The aim is a wheel that feels sustainable and aligned with what matters to you now.
