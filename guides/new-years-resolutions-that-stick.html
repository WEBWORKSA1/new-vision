Goal Setting

New Year's Resolutions That Stick: 9 Research-Backed Rules

By the New.Vision editorial teamUpdated November 16, 20268 min read

Most resolutions fade by February. Here are nine practical rules, grounded in behavioural research, for setting resolutions you'll still be keeping in December.

Key takeaways
  • Resolvers do better than people who simply want to change — so do set them.
  • Approach goals ('start X') tend to outperform avoidance goals ('stop Y').
  • Plan for the first slip in advance; it's coming.

Are resolutions worth it?

Yes. In a classic study, people who made New Year's resolutions were far more likely to report success six months later than people with similar goals who didn't make a resolution — roughly 46% versus 4% (Norcross, Mrykalo & Blagys, 2002). The fresh start of a new year gives a genuine motivational boost (Dai, Milkman & Riis, 2014). The trick is setting resolutions in a way that survives February.

1. Choose fewer resolutions

One to three. Each additional goal competes for the same limited willpower and attention.

2. Frame them as "approach" goals

A large Swedish study of over 1,000 people found that those with approach-oriented resolutions ("start eating a homemade lunch") were significantly more successful than those with avoidance-oriented ones ("stop eating junk food") (Oscarsson et al., 2020).

3. Make them specific

"Get fit" becomes "Strength train Monday, Wednesday and Friday at 7am for 30 minutes." Use our Goal Planner to turn any resolution into a SMART goal.

4. Shrink the first step

Make the starting action so small you can't say no: one push-up, one page, one sentence. Momentum matters more than intensity in January.

5. Attach it to an existing routine

"After I pour my morning coffee, I'll write my three priorities." Anchoring a new behaviour to an existing cue helps it become automatic.

6. Write an if-then plan for your biggest obstacle

Implementation intentions — "If X happens, then I'll do Y" — are among the best-supported tools for follow-through (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006).

7. Track it visibly

A calendar with crosses, a habit app, or the progress slider in your New.Vision goal list. Seeing a streak builds motivation to keep it.

8. Plan for the slip

You will miss a day. Decide now: "Never miss twice." A single miss is an accident; two in a row is the start of a new habit.

9. Get support

Tell one person, find an accountability partner, join a challenge, or work with a coach. Social support is consistently linked to better follow-through. If you want structured help, book a free Vision Strategy Session.

Frequently asked questions

When do most people give up on resolutions?

Many surveys point to mid-to-late January, which is why planning for early obstacles matters so much.

Is it too late to set a resolution in February?

No. Any meaningful date — a Monday, a birthday, the start of a month — can create a fresh start.
