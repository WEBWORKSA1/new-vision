Money Vision Board: How to Visualise and Plan Your Financial Goals

By the New.Vision editorial teamUpdated November 16, 20268 min read

Make a money vision board that's tied to real numbers: emergency fund, debt payoff, investing and big purchases — with simple formulas and milestone tracking.

Key takeaways
  • Every money tile needs a number and a date.
  • Automate the behaviour so the board isn't relying on willpower.
  • Celebrate milestones — 25%, 50%, 75% — not just the finish line.

Why money goals benefit from a visual

Financial goals are abstract: numbers in an app. A visual reminder of why you're saving — the home, the freedom, the trip, the calm — helps you make better trade-offs in the moment, such as skipping an impulse purchase. But the image must be paired with numbers, or it's just daydreaming.

Pick your money goals

Common, high-impact money goals include:

  • Emergency fund — often suggested as 3–6 months of essential expenses.
  • High-interest debt payoff — credit cards first, usually.
  • Regular investing — for example, a fixed amount every payday into a retirement or index fund account.
  • A big purchase — home deposit, car, education.
  • Income growth — a raise, new clients or a second income stream.
  • Giving — supporting causes you care about.

Turn each goal into a monthly number

Use this simple formula for any savings goal:

Monthly amount = (Target − Current savings) ÷ Months remaining

Example: a $9,000 emergency fund, starting from $1,800, in 12 months = ($9,000 − $1,800) ÷ 12 = $600 per month. If that's too much, extend the deadline or reduce the target — then commit.

Design the tiles

For each goal, create a tile with an image that represents the feeling (security, freedom, adventure), the target number and the date. For example: 🛟 "$9,000 safety net — Dec 31". Build it in the Vision Board Maker using the "Money Goals" template.

Automate the plan

The most reliable way to hit a savings number is to remove the decision. Set automatic transfers on payday into separate, named accounts ("Safety Net", "Japan 2027"). Many banks let you name sub-accounts — matching them to your board tiles makes the connection obvious.

Track milestones

Add progress to each tile at 25%, 50% and 75%. Our Goal Planner creates quarterly milestones and calendar check-ins automatically, and you can slide progress forward as you go.

Supporting habits

  • A 20-minute monthly "money date" to review spending and progress.
  • A 48-hour rule for non-essential purchases over a set amount.
  • An annual review of subscriptions and insurance.
  • Learning: one book or course on personal finance each quarter.

Frequently asked questions

How much should my emergency fund be?

A common guideline is 3–6 months of essential expenses, but the right amount depends on job stability, dependants and other factors. A licensed adviser can help you decide.

Should I pay off debt or save first?

Many people build a small starter emergency fund, then focus on high-interest debt. Your situation may differ — consider professional advice.
