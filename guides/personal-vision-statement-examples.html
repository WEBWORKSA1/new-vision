How to Write a Personal Vision Statement (With 25 Examples)

By the New.Vision editorial teamUpdated November 16, 20267 min read

What a personal vision statement is, how it differs from a mission statement, a 5-step writing process and 25 examples for different life stages.

Key takeaways
  • A vision statement describes the future you're building; a mission describes how you'll live day to day.
  • Keep it to one to three sentences you could remember under pressure.
  • Review it yearly — your vision should evolve as you do.
In this guide
  1. Vision vs mission
  2. Five steps to write yours
  3. A simple formula
  4. 25 personal vision statement examples
  5. Put it to work

Vision vs mission

A personal vision statement describes the future you want — who you'll be and what your life will look like. A personal mission statement describes how you'll act every day to get there. Think of vision as the destination and mission as the way you travel.

Five steps to write yours

  1. Reflect on peak moments. When have you felt most alive and proud? What were you doing?
  2. Name your top values. Pick three to five, such as freedom, family, mastery, health, generosity, adventure.
  3. Imagine 10 years out. Describe an ordinary day in your ideal life: work, people, place, health, contribution.
  4. Draft freely, then cut to the most powerful one to three sentences.
  5. Test it. Read it aloud. Does it make you sit up straighter? If not, keep editing.

A simple formula

"I am [who you want to be], living [how/where], so that [impact or meaning]."

25 personal vision statement examples

Career & purpose

  1. To be a trusted leader who builds teams where people do the best work of their lives.
  2. To create technology that makes healthcare simpler for families everywhere.
  3. To run a profitable, calm business that gives me freedom and gives my clients real results.
  4. To become a respected expert in my field who teaches generously.
  5. To use design to make everyday life a little more beautiful and a lot less confusing.

Family & relationships

  1. To raise kind, curious children in a home full of laughter and honest conversation.
  2. To be the friend people call first — reliable, present and fun.
  3. To build a marriage rooted in respect, adventure and shared growth.
  4. To be the person who keeps our family connected across distance and generations.
  5. To love well and be easy to love.

Health & wellbeing

  1. To live with energy, strength and calm into my 90s.
  2. To treat my body as a partner, not a project.
  3. To be someone who moves every day and enjoys it.
  4. To meet stress with steadiness and recover with grace.
  5. To model healthy habits for the people around me.

Growth & creativity

  1. To never stop learning, and to share what I learn.
  2. To make art that helps people feel less alone.
  3. To write the books I wish I'd read sooner.
  4. To be brave enough to begin and disciplined enough to finish.
  5. To grow a little wiser and kinder every year.

Freedom & contribution

  1. To achieve financial independence so I can choose work that matters.
  2. To live simply, travel widely and give generously.
  3. To leave every place and person better than I found them.
  4. To build a life I don't need a holiday from.
  5. To use my privilege and skills to open doors for others.

Put it to work

Place your statement at the centre of your vision board — use the title field in the Vision Board Maker — and read it before your weekly review.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a personal vision statement be?

One to three sentences. Short enough to remember, specific enough to guide choices.

Can my vision statement change?

Yes. Review it each year or after major life events.
